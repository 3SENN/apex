## The marks

- the name **Atelier** for the browser;
- the **Atelier logo**, the **sprout mark** and the app icons in `branding/official/`;
- the six **Atelier characters**, **Pip**, **Juniper**, **Bolt**, **Mochi**, **Sage** and **Pepper**: their names, looks
  and personalities. Pip is also the character in the logo.

## Official and unofficial branding

Like Firefox, Atelier has two brandings in this repository:

- **Official branding** (`branding/official/`): the Atelier name, logo, sprout mark and icons, with Pip as the default
  companion and all six characters offered. Only builds made and published by the Atelier project use it.
- **Unofficial branding** (`branding/unofficial/`): the name **Summit**, plain icons and a neutral default companion
  named Companion; none of the Atelier characters is offered. Every build from source uses it unless official branding is
  switched on, so you can build from source, change it and share your changes without touching the marks. Summit is a
  placeholder for source builds, not an Atelier trademark and not a name we claim; if you publish a fork, ship it under a
  name and icons of your own, with character designs of its own in place of the companion art
  ([branding/unofficial/README.md](branding/unofficial/README.md)).

## What you may do without asking

- **Pass on official builds**, as long as each copy is unmodified and complete (the installer or archive exactly as
  we published it), free of charge, not bundled with other software, with the installer unchanged, and a current
  release. Linking to [atelierbrowser.eu](https://atelierbrowser.eu) or the
  [releases page](https://github.com/3SENN/atelier/releases) instead is always fine.
- **Build from source** with the unofficial branding, change it, and share your changes under the MPL-2.0. Builds
  you share so others can test or review changes for Atelier (pull-request previews, testers, CI artifacts) are fine. A
  build you distribute as your own product (a fork) must use a name and icons of its own and its own character
  designs in place of the companion art ([ART-LICENSE.md](ART-LICENSE.md), section 3.1).
- **Say truthfully** that your project is based on Atelier or works with Atelier, as long as you don't suggest it is
  official or endorsed.
- **Write about Atelier**, review it, teach it and link to it, using the name, logo and characters to refer to the real
  thing.
- **Make packs, apps, companions and other content** and say that they are "for Atelier". Don't call them "official
  Atelier …" or present them as made or endorsed by the Atelier project.

## Characters made with Atelier

The [Atelier Companion Art License](ART-LICENSE.md) sets what you may do with the companion art; in short:

- **Create and share characters for Atelier.** Make companions from Atelier's parts and share them (for example as
  `.ateliercompanion` files), and put them in packs, apps and worlds made for Atelier, including ones you sell.
- **Your own character may be your brand's mascot inside Atelier.** A character you make, with a name and personality of
  your own, may represent your brand, product or organisation inside Atelier: for example as the companion of your pack,
  app or world.
- **Not outside Atelier.** Characters made from the companion art can't be a mascot, logo, icon or brand character in your
  own apps, websites, products, merchandise or services, or the identity of a fork or a new brand, and they can't be
  used in other software or in a fork you distribute. Showing them in screenshots, videos, reviews, tutorials and store
  listings about Atelier, or about things made for Atelier, is fine.
- **Atelier's six characters are never someone else's mascot.** Pip, Juniper, Bolt, Mochi, Sage and Pepper can't be the
  mascot of anyone else's brand, product or organisation, inside or outside Atelier.

## What needs permission

- Distributing a modified build, a fork or another product under the Atelier name, with the Atelier logo, sprout mark or
  icons, or with an Atelier character as its mascot or logo.
- Passing on official builds in any way the list above doesn't cover: for a fee, bundled with other software, with
  a changed installer, or as an old release.
- Using the marks, or a name confusingly similar to them, in a product name, company name, domain name or logo of
  your own. That includes names built on the mark, such as "Atelier-anything" or "Atelierium".
- Using a character made from the companion art in any way the section above doesn't cover.
